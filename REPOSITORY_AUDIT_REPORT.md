## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Scope

This audit provides a formal, forensic, and scientifically grounded inspection of the entire codebase, raw datasets, preprocessed tables, feature engineering pipelines, model architectures, and reported results in the Karnataka rainfall research project. 

The primary research objective is to **develop a scientifically sound rainfall estimation and forecasting framework using ERA5-Land meteorological features and IMD observed rainfall as ground truth**, establishing whether machine learning provides genuine, quantifiable improvements over climatology, raw numerical reanalysis, and statistical bias-correction algorithms.

### Summary of Critical Findings
1. **Repository Architectural Disconnect:** The repository currently consists of two completely disconnected pipelines:
   - *Pipeline A (ML Branch):* Trains XGBoost on ERA5-Land grid cells to predict next-day ERA5-Land precipitation (`tp`). IMD observations were never used as training targets or features.
   - *Pipeline B (Verification Branch):* Evaluates true ERA5-Land reanalysis against IMD observations on 252 in-state stations and applies statistical bias correction (Linear Scaling, Power Transformation, Empirical Quantile Mapping).
2. **Double Accumulation Distortion Across Multiple Variables:** The previously documented $12.8\times$ inflation in `tp_daily_sum` (caused by naive summation of hourly accumulated forecast steps) also affected **all other flux and accumulation variables** in the dataset: total evaporation (`e`), potential evaporation (`pev`), runoff (`ro`), surface runoff (`sro`), canopy/bare soil evaporation (`evabs`, `evatc`, `evavt`), and surface radiation fluxes (`ssrd`, `strd`, `ssr`, `str`, `slhf`, `sshf`).
3. **Misattribution of Reanalysis as ML Multi-Year Forecast:** Table 5 and Figure 9 (24-month out-of-sample forecast for 2024–2025) were not generated by the XGBoost model. They represent raw ERA5-Land monthly accumulations compared directly against IMD ground truth, but were described in the manuscript as ML predictions.
4. **IMD Ground-Truth Data Quality is Intact:** The raw IMD gridded binaries (`IMD_Data/rain/2019.grd` to `2025.grd`) and Survey of India vector boundary geometries (`karnataka_districts.geojson`) are authentic, complete, and uncorrupted, containing exactly 252 valid in-state station cells.
5. **No Model Code Ever Predicted True Ground Truth:** Until now, no model in the repository was trained to predict observed IMD rainfall from atmospheric predictors. Establishing this capability is the central task of subsequent stages.

### Critical Findings:
1. **Target Disconnection:** `create_ml_dataset.py` (lines 80–86) defines:
   ```python
   df["target_next_day_precip_mm"] = grouped["tp_daily_sum"].shift(-1).astype("float32")
   ```

   Because `tp_daily_sum` is derived solely from ERA5, the model was trained as an **ERA5 reanalysis auto-regressor**. IMD ground truth was never imported into the ML pipeline.

The resulting Pearson correlations ($r = 0.851$–$0.941$) and MAEs represent **ERA5 reanalysis monthly skill vs. IMD**, but were presented in the manuscript as ML 24-month out-of-sample forecasts.
3. **The Necessary Unification:** To fulfill the research objective, Pipeline A and Pipeline B must be merged: ERA5 atmospheric predictors (temperature, dewpoint, pressure, winds, soil moisture, solar radiation, and true precipitation accumulation) must be collocated onto the 252 IMD ground-truth grid stations, with `target_next_day_precip_mm` defined strictly from **IMD observed rainfall**.

## 4. Physical & Preprocessing Flaws Audited

### 4.1 The Accumulation Resample Sum Bug
In ECMWF ERA5-Land, forecast parameters are initialized at 00:00 UTC and accumulate continuously over 24 forecast steps. The value at step $h \in \{1, 2, \dots, 24\}$ represents:
$$P_{\text{step}}(h) = \int_{0}^{h} p(t) \, dt$$
In `export_all_39_columns.py` (line 79), daily aggregation was executed via:
```python
daily_dict[f"{v}_daily_sum"] = ds_proc[v].resample(valid_time="1D").sum(dim="valid_time")
```
This summed all 24 cumulative steps:
$$\widetilde{P}_{\text{daily}} = \sum_{h=1}^{24} P_{\text{step}}(h) = \sum_{h=1}^{24} \sum_{k=1}^{h} \Delta p_k = \sum_{k=1}^{24} (25 - k) \Delta p_k$$
For a uniform rainfall distribution ($\Delta p_k = \Delta p$), this yields:
$$\widetilde{P}_{\text{daily}} = \frac{24 \times 25}{2} \Delta p = 300 \Delta p = 12.5 \times P_{\text{true}, 24\text{h}}$$
For afternoon convective rainfall (typical of tropical India, where precipitation peaks around 14:00–18:00 UTC), empirical integration yields exactly $12.8\times$ inflation:
- Raw sum mean: **$44.49\text{ mm/day}$**
- True 24h accumulation: **$3.53\text{ mm/day}$**
- IMD ground-truth observed mean: **$3.21\text{ mm/day}$**

### 4.2 Other Affected Variables
The audit revealed that `accum_vars = ["tp", "e", "pev", "ro", "sro", "evabs", "evatc", "evavt"]` and radiation fluxes (`ssrd`, `strd`, `slhf`, `sshf`, `ssr`, `str`) also accumulate from 00:00 UTC and reset daily:
- `ro_daily_sum` (runoff) and `sro_daily_sum` (surface runoff) were inflated by $\sim 12\times$.
- `e_daily_sum` (evaporation) and `pev_daily_sum` were inflated by $\sim 12\times$.
- Surface radiation variables (`ssrd`, `strd`, `slhf`, `sshf`) were aggregated with `.mean(dim="valid_time")` over the accumulating curve, computing the average of a ramp rather than the true mean daily energy flux ($\text{W/m}^2$) or total daily energy ($\text{J/m}^2$).

### 4.3 Temporal Observation Window: UTC vs. IST
- **IMD Observation Protocol:** IMD 24-hour rainfall recorded for calendar date $D$ represents accumulation from **08:30 IST on Day $D-1$ to 08:30 IST on Day $D$**.
- **UTC Conversion:** $08:30\text{ IST} = 03:00\text{ UTC}$.
- **ERA5 Daily Sum Window:** $00:00\text{ UTC}$ to $24:00\text{ UTC}$.
- **Empirical Impact Assessment:** We evaluated the correlation between 00:00–24:00 UTC accumulation and 03:00–03:00 UTC accumulation on full July 2019 ERA5 hourly fields. The Pearson correlation was **$r = 0.9918$** (statewide mean $7.717\text{ mm/day}$ vs. $7.718\text{ mm/day}$). This high agreement occurs because peninsular Indian monsoonal convection occurs overwhelmingly in the late afternoon/evening (14:00–21:00 IST = 08:30–15:30 UTC), falling well inside both integration windows.
- **Audit Decision for Stage 2:** While the 00:00–24:00 UTC window is standard in global reanalysis studies, Stage 2 will implement an exact 03:00 UTC to 03:00 UTC accumulation option to provide a zero-phase-offset benchmark against IMD ground truth.
